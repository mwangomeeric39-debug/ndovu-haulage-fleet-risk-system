# Ndovu Haulage — Fleet Risk Intelligence System

**An End-to-End Data Engineering, Business Intelligence & AI Project**

## 1. Project Overview

Ndovu Haulage is a fictional Kenyan trucking company operating a fleet of 40 trucks. Managing vehicle availability, fuel consumption, maintenance, driver performance, and delivery deadlines requires more than traditional reporting.

This project demonstrates how to build a Fleet Risk Intelligence System that transforms operational data into actionable business intelligence.

The solution integrates data engineering, SQL-based risk detection, Power BI reporting, workflow automation, and AI-generated explanations to help fleet managers identify emerging operational risks and prioritize appropriate action.

**Core principle:** Move beyond reporting what happened to identifying what needs attention and supporting the next business action.

## 2. Business Problem

Fleet operators can face several interconnected challenges:

- **Unexpected breakdowns:** Vehicles may develop faults that disrupt deliveries and increase repair costs.
- **Fuel irregularities:** Unusual fuel consumption or discrepancies between fuel-card transactions and tank measurements may indicate waste, leakage, recording errors, or possible fraud.
- **Poor dispatch decisions:** Vehicles with unresolved faults may be assigned trips before their condition is reviewed.
- **Delivery delays:** Excessive idle time, inefficient routes, and vehicle problems can affect delivery performance.
- **Limited operational visibility:** Managers may struggle to identify high-risk vehicles and prioritize cases from multiple data sources.
- **Slow follow-up:** Risk information may not reach the responsible person quickly enough to support timely intervention.

The project addresses these challenges by connecting data processing, risk detection, reporting, and operational follow-up in one workflow.

## 3. Project Objectives

1. Build a structured data pipeline for fleet operations.
2. Transform raw operational records into reliable, business-ready datasets.
3. Detect potential vehicle, fuel, maintenance, and delivery risks using explainable rules.
4. Present fleet performance and risk indicators in Power BI.
5. Automate selected alerts and notifications using n8n.
6. Use Gemini AI to summarize detected risks and explain the supporting evidence.
7. Support human review and follow-up through alert and case records.

### How the architecture works

**Step 1 — Data ingestion**

Fleet records such as vehicle telemetry, trip information, fuel transactions, driver records, and maintenance events enter the data platform.

**Step 2 — Data cleaning**

The Silver layer standardizes and validates the data so downstream calculations use consistent records.

**Step 3 — Business modeling**

The Gold layer organizes the cleaned data into dimensions, facts, intermediate models, and reporting marts.

**Step 4 — Risk detection**

SQL rules evaluate operational conditions and identify cases requiring attention.

**Step 5 — Reporting and automation**

Power BI provides visibility into fleet performance and risk. n8n retrieves eligible alerts and orchestrates notifications and follow-up workflows.

**Step 6 — AI-assisted explanation**

Gemini converts the evidence produced by the risk engine into a concise explanation for the recipient.

**Step 7 — Human action**

A responsible fleet manager reviews the evidence, investigates the case, and determines the appropriate action.

*Important design principle:* SQL rules determine whether a risk condition exists. Gemini explains the supplied evidence; it is not the authoritative risk detector.

## 6. Data Engineering Architecture

### 6.1 Bronze Layer — Raw Data

The Bronze layer stores ingested source records with minimal transformation.

Potential source datasets include:

- Vehicle master records
- Driver records
- Trip and dispatch records
- GPS and vehicle telemetry
- Fuel-card transactions
- Tank sensor readings
- Maintenance and repair records
- Route and delivery information

**Purpose:** Preserve the source data and provide a foundation for downstream processing and investigation.

### 6.2 Silver Layer — Cleaned Data

The Silver layer prepares the source records for reliable analytical use.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Standardizing data types and timestamps
- Cleaning identifiers and categorical values
- Handling missing and invalid records
- Identifying duplicates
- Validating relationships between vehicles, drivers, trips, and transactions
- Preparing consistent operational records for analysis

**Purpose:** Make the data trustworthy enough for business calculations and risk detection.

### 6.3 Gold Layer — Business-Ready Data

The Gold layer contains models designed around fleet management questions.

**Dimensions**

| Model | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `gold.dim_vehicle` | Vehicle attributes, registration, make, model, capacity, and depot |
| `gold.dim_driver` | Driver reference information |
| `gold.dim_route` | Route attributes and reference information |
| `gold.dim_date` | Calendar attributes for time-based analysis |

**Intermediate and fact models**

| Model | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `gold.int_trip_metrics` | Trip-level metrics such as idle time and moving speed |
| `gold.ref_burn_baseline` | Reference fuel-consumption benchmarks |
| `gold.fact_trip` | Trip performance and operational outcomes |
| `gold.fact_fuel_txn` | Fuel-card transaction records |
| `gold.fact_fuel_event` | Fuel-related events and detected irregularities |

**Operational marts and KPIs**

| Model | Purpose |
|---|---|

Exact schemas and refresh behavior depend on the implemented SQL definitions.

## 7. Risk Detection Engine

The risk engine converts business rules and operational measurements into cases that managers can investigate.

### 7.1 Vehicle Health and Maintenance Risk

Vehicle health indicators are used to highlight trucks that may need inspection or maintenance.

Possible evidence includes:

- Active or unresolved fault records
- Recent breakdowns
- Deteriorating vehicle-health indicators
- Maintenance history
- Abnormal operating conditions

A failure-risk score can help prioritize vehicles for review over a defined prediction horizon, such as the next 48 hours.

**Business outcome:** Help fleet managers prioritize preventive inspection and reduce avoidable disruption.

A risk score is an estimate, not a guarantee that a vehicle will fail.

### 7.2 Fuel Anomaly Detection

Fuel consumption is evaluated against an appropriate baseline rather than relying on one fixed threshold for every truck.

The baseline can account for factors such as:

- Route
- Vehicle make and model
- Trip load or operating conditions
- Historical fuel consumption

Fuel-card transaction volumes can also be compared with available tank-sensor measurements.

Potential indicators include unusually high consumption, unexplained discrepancies, or suspicious refueling patterns.

**Business outcome:** Identify transactions and trips that warrant fuel-efficiency checks or further investigation.

An anomaly is not proof of theft. Sensor errors, route conditions, missing data, and recording differences must also be considered.

### 7.3 Dispatch Readiness

Dispatch logic evaluates whether a vehicle should be classified as:

- **CLEAR:** No blocking condition was identified by the configured rules.
- **RESTRICT:** A condition requires additional review or a limitation.
- **LOCK:** A configured blocking condition prevents normal dispatch approval.

The rules should use explicit operational criteria, including relevant unresolved faults and vehicle-readiness conditions.

**Business outcome:** Support safer, more consistent dispatch decisions.

These classifications are decision-support outputs; actual dispatch restrictions should follow the company's approved operational and safety procedures.

### 7.4 Delivery and Trip Performance

Trip-level metrics can reveal:

- Excessive idle time
- Low or unusual moving speeds
- Delivery SLA breaches
- Breakdown-related interruptions
- Route inefficiencies

**Business outcome:** Help operations teams investigate delivery delays and identify opportunities to improve fleet utilization.

## 8. Power BI Reporting

The Power BI layer provides an operational view of fleet health, performance, and emerging risks.

### Executive Overview

Designed to answer: *What requires management attention today?*

Potential KPIs include:

- Total fleet size
- Vehicles classified as high risk
- Average vehicle-health score
- Dispatch-ready vehicles
- High-priority operational alerts
- Fuel alerts requiring investigation

### Fuel Risk Analysis

Designed to answer: *Where are fuel-related irregularities occurring?*

Possible analysis includes:

- Fuel consumption against baseline
- Fuel-card and tank-measurement discrepancies
- Fuel events by vehicle and route
- Trends in anomalies over time

### Fleet Operations

Designed to answer: *Which operational issues are affecting vehicle availability and delivery performance?*

Possible analysis includes:

- Vehicle availability
- Maintenance and breakdown trends
- Trip performance and delivery delays
- Driver and route performance
- Dispatch readiness

Power BI provides visibility into the evidence and trends. The risk engine and automation workflow handle detection and notification responsibilities.

## 9. Workflow Automation with n8n

The n8n workflow connects detected risk cases to notification and follow-up processes.

### Workflow sequence

1. A manual or scheduled trigger starts the workflow.
2. n8n queries the relevant Databricks SQL endpoint or alert queue.
3. Eligible alert records are retrieved.
4. The workflow prepares the evidence for explanation.
5. Gemini generates a concise explanation grounded in that evidence.
6. An email notification is sent to the intended recipient.
7. Alert and case records are updated where configured.

### Alert information

A useful notification should include:

- Vehicle or asset identifier
- Risk category and priority
- Time of detection
- Supporting evidence
- Potential business impact, where calculated
- Recommended next step
- Responsible owner, where configured

### Tracking and follow-up

Supporting tables such as `gold.alert_log` and `gold.risk_case` can record notification history and case status.

A production implementation should also handle duplicate notifications, failed workflow runs, missing evidence, API errors, and retries.

AI-generated recommendations should remain advisory until reviewed by the responsible person.

## 10. Example Operational Scenario

Consider a truck whose fuel consumption is significantly higher than its historical baseline for comparable operating conditions.

1. Fuel and trip records are cleaned in Silver.
2. Gold models calculate the relevant trip metrics and fuel-consumption baseline.
3. The SQL risk engine detects a deviation and creates an alert.
4. Power BI displays the vehicle and supporting fuel indicators.
5. n8n retrieves the eligible alert.
6. Gemini summarizes the observed deviation and the evidence supplied by SQL.
7. An email informs the responsible fleet manager.
8. The manager reviews the fuel transaction, route, and sensor records before deciding whether further investigation is needed.

This scenario demonstrates how a data pipeline can connect operational evidence to a human decision instead of stopping at a chart.

## 11. Business Value

The system is designed to support:

- Earlier visibility into potential maintenance issues
- More consistent vehicle-readiness reviews
- Faster identification of unusual fuel patterns
- Improved monitoring of delivery performance
- Better prioritization of operational cases
- More traceable alerts and follow-up
- Reduced manual effort in routine monitoring and communication

Actual savings, reductions in breakdowns, and improvements in fuel efficiency must be measured against a documented baseline after operational deployment. They should not be assumed from the existence of the system alone.

## 12. Data Quality and Validation

Before relying on the outputs operationally, validate the following:

- Fuel-consumption calculations when distance is zero or missing
- Sufficient historical records for reliable baselines
- Duplicate fuel-event joins that could inflate totals
- Consistent definitions of vehicle availability
- Correct treatment of missing telemetry and sensor readings
- Meaningful interpretation of driver scorecards
- Correct joins between vehicles, drivers, routes, trips, and fuel transactions
- Risk thresholds against real operational examples
- Alert deduplication and case-status transitions
- Notification failures and audit logging

Where source data is incomplete, the system should surface the limitation instead of presenting uncertain results as confirmed facts.

## 13. Security and Governance

For a production deployment, the following controls are recommended:

- Use least-privilege access to Databricks data and SQL endpoints.
- Store API tokens and credentials in approved secret-management facilities.
- Restrict access to driver, vehicle, and operational records by role.
- Avoid including unnecessary sensitive information in AI prompts and emails.
- Log alert generation, delivery, and case updates.
- Keep human review in place for safety-critical or financially consequential decisions.
- Document the business meaning and limitations of every risk score.

## 14. Project Limitations

This project is a portfolio demonstration using a fictional trucking company and illustrative operational data. Its findings do not establish the performance of a real fleet deployment.

Key limitations include:

- Synthetic data may not capture every real-world operating condition.
- Anomaly detection can produce false positives.
- Prediction quality depends on the quality and quantity of historical data.
- AI explanations depend on the accuracy and completeness of the supplied evidence.
- Operational outcomes depend on whether alerts are reviewed and acted upon.

Before real deployment, the system would require validation with actual fleet data, operational stakeholder review, access controls, monitoring, and measured business outcomes.

## 15. Future Improvements

Potential next steps include:

- Calibrating risk thresholds against real maintenance outcomes
- Measuring precision and recall for predictive alerts
- Improving fuel baselines for routes with limited history
- Introducing alert severity and escalation policies
- Tracking acknowledgement and resolution times
- Measuring prevented downtime and fuel-cost variance
- Adding operational feedback to improve rules and models
- Deploying monitored, scheduled production refreshes

## 16. Conclusion

The Ndovu Haulage Fleet Risk Intelligence System demonstrates how data engineering, business intelligence, risk detection, and workflow automation can work together to support operational decisions.

Its value is not limited to visualizing fleet performance. The architecture connects raw operational records to business-ready data, identifies conditions that require attention, explains the supporting evidence, and helps deliver that information to the people responsible for acting on it.

**The end-to-end principle:**

**Data → Transformation → Risk Detection → Decision Support → Alert → Human Action**

The project showcases a practical approach to building business intelligence systems around operational problems rather than isolated dashboards.
